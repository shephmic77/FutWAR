Research prototype · no approved formula

Current model direction

Develop expected match points as the first candidate outcome scale; retain expected goal difference as a later comparator. Treat adjusted lineup impact as a candidate addition only for the team-outcome signal action values leave unexplained. Measure teammate uplift separately when it can be tied to goal difference. Compare components out of sample and report overlap and uncertainty; do not add raw overlapping estimates. Keep goalkeeper measurement separate until it can be calibrated to a shared outcome scale.

Player-link evidence · 4,500 sampled event actors checked against independent match lineups

A 2017–18 cross-provider sample found 2,941 exact actor-name matches in same-team, positive-minute OpenPlay lineups. Another 1,169 were unique name-variant candidates, and 390 stayed unresolved. Sampling was balanced across league and event family, so these counts are a sample description, not an overall accuracy estimate.

Lineup presence supports that the player appeared for that team; it does not prove they made the specific event or resolve every player-season identity. La Liga showed a much higher reliance on name variants, so source-specific identity work remains open. No player score is released from this audit.

Independent shot check · 37,425 cross-provider event pairs

OpenPlay and Wyscout shots were matched by fixture, team, half, and event time, without using player names. The pairing covered 92.5% of the smaller shot-event total across 1,826 fixtures. Exact actor-name agreement was 58.9% overall, with a large league gap: 18.1% in La Liga and 74.7% in the Bundesliga.

This supports that the feeds often record the same shot sequence, but it does not prove who took each shot. The name-variant sensitivity adds possible matches, not verified identities. It covers shots in 2017–18 only; no player scores are released from this check.

Shot-actor lineup plausibility · 2017–18 only

Of 40,461 Wyscout shots in 1,826 linked fixtures, 24,431 actor names matched a unique OpenPlay lineup name exactly; 12,247 more were constrained name-variant candidates. Of those 36,678 name links, 36,444 fell inside the recorded stint, 229 were outside but within a four-minute boundary allowance, and 5 were more than four minutes outside. Another 3,783 shot actors remained unresolved.

This is a plausibility screen: being listed for the team and on the pitch means a player could have taken a shot, not that they did. The lineup table comes from the OpenPlay release, name variants are candidates, and the broad timing allowance can hide short boundary conflicts. We manually reviewed all five >4-minute flags: independent match reports place the players on earlier than the OpenPlay lineup start, so these are stint-timing conflicts, not confirmed shot-actor errors. This review does not validate actor identity or player value.

Latest player-signal check · 2025–26

The player-form candidate did not transfer

Not a final test · no player score

Prior player-stat summaries helped a little in older season tests, but the candidate had higher overall errors than team context in 2025–26, and log loss worsened in each of the five leagues. Across all fixtures, expected-points error rose by 0.051; Brier score rose by 0.011; log loss rose by 0.016. Higher means worse.

This tests whether a team’s starting-player summaries help predict the match result. It does not prove an individual player’s value. The season was already inspected during development, so this is a warning and not the untouched final test. The player signal stays experimental; we will not tune it to this season.

Multi-season lineup check · 2018–19 to 2024–25

Starting-player data added almost no stable forecast signal

Exploratory check · no player score

In a model tuned only on earlier seasons, adding the starting XI improved match-outcome log loss in 3 of 7 future-season folds. The average change was just −0.00033 log-loss points (lower is better) against a context model near 1.0. This is too small and inconsistent to justify adding lineup impact to the score today.

The test asks whether knowing the selected starters helps forecast team results. It does not establish how many points any player caused, and it does not yet test lineup impact on top of validated action values. The score remains under construction.

Action plus lineup · six chronological season folds

Neither signal improved the shared match forecast

Exploratory · not WAR validation

Across 2,632 held-out matches, adding action candidates to halftime context raised expected-points error by 0.00218. Adding starting-player identities on top of actions raised it by 0.01338 (95% paired match interval: +0.00995 to +0.01687). Lower error is better; the lineup addition worsened error in all six season folds.

This is a test of whether the candidate signals help forecast team results. It does not prove that players or actions lack value. The season folds were already used in development, and player identities have not been independently verified well enough to treat these forecasts as individual contribution or WAR.

Secondary outcome check · goal difference · six chronological season folds

Action and lineup candidates also raised goal-margin error

Exploratory · not WAR validation

Across 2,632 held-out matches, adding starting-player identities on top of action candidates raised final goal-difference error by 0.0145 goals (95% paired match interval: +0.0072 to +0.0218). Lower error is better. This was worse in five of six season folds; 2024–25 was a small improvement.

League point estimates were worse in all five leagues, though the league-level 95% intervals excluded zero only for Ligue 1 and Serie A. The action candidates alone also raised error over halftime context by 0.0053 goals pooled, with intervals crossing zero in four leagues. Goal difference is the realized final goal margin, not expected goals (xG). These are team forecasts, not evidence that any individual player added or cost goals. The result does not validate player credit, lineup impact, or WAR.

Action-credit sensitivity

2017–18 shot-ending sequence pilot · attacking-only diagnostic · not a player ranking or approved credit rule

The direct-actor and equal-participant methods allocate the same modeled sequence total, but player ordering differs. Whiskers show 95% match-cluster bootstrap intervals from 1,000 fixture resamples; each cutoff keeps its full-sample cohort fixed.

Minimum sequencesPlayers includedSpearman ρ · observed; bootstrap median [95% interval]Rank movement · observed; bootstrap median [95% interval]

Observed across all 1,970 IDs, Spearman ρ is 0.812 and median rank movement is 171 places; at 25+ sequences, these are 0.881 and 66.5 places among 286 IDs. Match-bootstrap medians at those cutoffs are 0.786 [0.763, 0.810] and 144 [124, 170], then 0.862 [0.829, 0.890] and 76 [61, 94.5]. Neither method is shown to be more accurate. Sequence boundaries are proxies, the player IDs are not reconciled to the eight-season site profiles, and the sample omits most defending and goalkeeper actions.

What an “action change” contains

Consecutive-event replay · expected-points accounting diagnostic · not isolated player credit

Clock, own events & opponent events all move state

Each successive event updates both teams’ expected-points state. The observed step includes elapsed time since the previous event. The clock-only line is a counterfactual that freezes the previous event features while advancing time; the remaining event-update residual depends on that assumption.

The mean absolute components are separate diagnostics; they should not be added as if they formed an additive share. “Target-team event” means the target team’s expected-points state changed while its event was current, not that the player caused the change. Adjusted-lineup feasibility

OpenPlay · 2018–19 to 2024–25 held-out seasons · match points

No stable lineup gain

Across 14,328 matches with valid starting XIs, adding player-presence features did not reliably improve expected-points forecasts beyond pre-match team context. At a fixed exploratory shrinkage setting, context performed better in all seven future-season folds. Selecting shrinkage using earlier seasons only made the average difference nearly zero and inconsistent across folds.

Fixed shrinkageStarting XI: +0.0185 log loss · context better in 7/7 seasons
Earlier-season tuningStarting XI: −0.00033 average difference · mixed
Player coverage11.4%–19.2% of held-out starter slots were unseen IDs
InterpretationAssociation only · not action residual or player impact

Positive differences mean the lineup model had higher log loss. Realized-minute features are post-match and can reflect substitutions or match events. This result does not rule out better adjusted models; it does not estimate player value or teammate uplift. The required action-only versus action-plus-lineup comparison remains open.
