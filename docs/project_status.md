## Adjusted-lineup signal — 2026-11-05

A new seven-fold OpenPlay walk-forward compared a pre-match results context model with added starting-XI identity features and, separately, realized player-minute exposure. Across 14,328 valid matches (six lineup-invalid matches excluded), both additions increased expected-points log loss in every held-out season at fixed exploratory shrinkage. The mean increase was 0.01848 for starting-XI features; five of seven match-bootstrap intervals excluded zero in that direction. With C chosen only on each fold's latest earlier validation season, the average starting-XI difference was near zero (−0.00033), with mixed directions and only two intervals favoring the lineup model; the realized-minute difference was also near zero (−0.00027) and mixed. Between 11.4% and 19.2% of held-out starting-XI slots were IDs unseen in the preceding training seasons. Realized-minutes features are post-match and can encode substitutions or response to the score. This does not validate player impact or teammate uplift. The current lineup signal has no reliable incremental gain; the required action-only versus action-plus-lineup comparison remains open because current action transitions are not a validated, continuous player-match signal. No coefficients or player scores were written. Reproduce with `py scripts\run_openplay_lineup_presence_walkforward.py`; see [full methods and results](openplay_lineup_presence_walkforward_2026-11-05.md).

## Multi-season 3/5/10-event transition sensitivity — 2026-11-05

A seven-fold expanding-season comparison now measures endpoint movement across 3, 5, and 10 later event rows on 12,504 held-out fixtures. Equal-season mean absolute clock-adjusted residuals were 0.00324, 0.00537, and 0.01072 expected points for state-only, compared with 0.02094, 0.02446, and 0.03044 for event-context. The matched model-gap estimates and 95% intervals are 0.01770 [0.01761, 0.01779], 0.01909 [0.01900, 0.01920], and 0.01971 [0.01961, 0.01981] at 3/5/10 rows; the fixture bootstrap weights matches by their sampled transition counts within season and gives each season equal weight. These 1,000-resample intervals condition on fitted models. The event-context/state-only scale gap narrows as windows lengthen but remains material; both scales rise with the horizon. The endpoint contains all intervening events from both teams and the residual subtracts a hypothetical frozen-event clock scenario, so neither series measures an isolated action or supports player credit. 2024–25 has been used during method development and is not an untouched final test. The local ignored outputs are reproduced by `py scripts\run_openplay_action_horizon_sensitivity.py`; see the [methods and results](openplay_action_horizon_sensitivity_2026-11-05.md). The WAR page now displays this eight-season sensitivity alongside full-window counts, replacing its older one-season horizon values.

## Position-minute evidence update — 2026-11-05

An expanding-season OpenPlay formation-slot audit now tests whether source lineup kickoff-position labels can be predicted from formation shape and slot using only earlier seasons. Across 35 held-out league-season cells, 273,756/273,812 eligible labels matched (99.98%) at the exploratory 20-training-label / 95%-purity screen; eligible label coverage was 99.51%. But formation duration still fails the lineup-minute comparison in 24,272/372,738 held-out played player-match rows by more than five minutes. The median formation-minus-lineup difference is -2 minutes, the maximum absolute difference is 95, and cell-level within-five-minute agreement ranges down to 78.85%. Of 12,508 matches checked, 189 have expanded timelines above 105 minutes despite no `extra_time` flag; excluding them gives 94.12% within-five-minute agreement in the remaining matches. This is a review group, not 189 proven bad clocks: reports confirm long stoppages in several examples, while Nîmes–Monaco 2019–20 is one independently confirmed bad clock. Combining supported mapped slots with the five-minute tolerance yields 90.39% of all held-out lineup minutes, but this is diagnostic source-label coverage, not validated FutWAR role exposure. Substitute agreement is lower than starter agreement. Treat discrepant exposure as unknown; the actual-position-minute gate remains open. Full methods, sample match checks and references are in [the position-slot audit](openplay_position_slot_audit_2026-11-05.md). The reproducible script writes aggregate-only, ignored outputs and does not alter raw data.

A follow-up, explicitly **sensitivity-only**, compared 5-, 10-, 15-, and 30-minute caps for extending the final valid formation spell to the last recorded event minute (timeline at most 105, not flagged extra time, final spell at minute 85 or later). The 15-minute cap changed 24,592 spells and added 71,563 aggregate minutes; within-five-minute agreement rose to 98.30% overall and 99.01% for matches whose timelines end by minute 105. Supported mapped-slot minutes within tolerance rose to 97.80% of the lineup-minute total. The 189 >105-minute matches remain untouched for match-specific review; reporting confirms both legitimate interruptions and one independently confirmed bad clock among examples reviewed. Because the last event is not an official full-time timestamp and the remaining cell minimum is 93.67%, this is not adopted as a correction and the position-minute gate remains open. Reproduction: `py scripts\audit_openplay_stoppage_extension_sensitivity.py`; aggregate outputs are ignored locally. See the [position-slot audit](openplay_position_slot_audit_2026-11-05.md) and the detailed [sensitivity comparison](openplay_stoppage_extension_sensitivity_2026-11-05.md).

The same time-forward role map links a unique keeper slot to 300,005/314,337 eligible shots (95.44%), including 102,129/107,066 shots on target (95.39%). Most unassigned shots fall outside a mapped keeper interval (13,449 records); 882 have no mapped interval and one is ambiguous. On-target linkage is 90.59% in the 189 matches whose event timeline exceeds 105 minutes, versus 95.47% at or below 105; the longer-match group includes legitimate long stoppages as well as at least one bad clock. This supports a local goalkeeper-specific data linkage prototype, not shot-stopping value or a common keeper/outfield scale. Assignment accuracy has no independent representative spot-check yet. See [the keeper-shot linkage audit](openplay_keeper_shot_linkage_2026-11-05.md).

The matching **sensitivity-only** endpoint comparison improves keeper interval coverage as well: with a 15-minute cap, a unique keeper interval covers 98.82% of shots and 98.88% of shots on target (99.03% of on-target shots in matches at or below minute 105); 2,825 shots remain outside a mapped interval, 882 have no mapped interval, and one is ambiguous. The over-105 group remains at 90.59% on-target coverage. This still measures coverage, not accuracy, and inherits the unverified last-event proxy and GK slot mapping. Do not calculate keeper values from this linkage. See the [keeper endpoint sensitivity report](openplay_keeper_stoppage_sensitivity_2026-11-05.md).

The 2026-11-05 [event-context expected-points walk-forward](openplay_action_value_walkforward_2026-11-05.md) includes home/away and strictly prior within-season team form. Event context had a small mean Brier improvement over state-only (event minus state: -0.0000479 across folds; -0.0000476 match-weighted): lower Brier in six seasons, with four intervals favoring event context, one favoring state-only, and two crossing zero. Mean absolute event-context transition was 0.01596 expected points (95% match-cluster CI 0.01587–0.01606) versus 0.00108 for the state-only residual (95% CI 0.00105–0.00110). The scale gap appears in all five leagues. Feature ablations show that removing event type reduces the residual about 5% (0.01509; 95% CI 0.01500–0.01518), removing outcome has almost no effect (0.01586; 95% CI 0.01577–0.01596), and removing coarse location reduces it about 7.5% (0.01476; 95% CI 0.01467–0.01485). This remains team-state association, not player credit; no player IDs or credits were used.

A separate expected-goal-difference check on the same seven chronological folds produced a tiny pooled RMSE improvement with event context (-0.00116 goals/state; 95% match-cluster CI [-0.00166, -0.00066]); pooled MAE uncertainty crossed zero. League RMSE intervals favored event context only in the Premier League and Serie A. Yet the event-context mean absolute clock-adjusted transition residual was 0.02002 goals [0.01995, 0.02009], versus 0.00271 [0.00267, 0.00276] for state-only. This is a second warning that forecast fit and calibration do not justify treating the transition scale as action value. Expected points remains the chosen primary target; goal difference is a comparison check. Full per-league results, calibration, fold diagnostics, and PowerShell reproduction commands are in the [walk-forward report](openplay_action_value_walkforward_2026-11-05.md). Outputs remain local/ignored; no player-level data or scores were produced.

The result confirms feature-driven movement in the transition scale; even the smallest ablation residual remains about 13 times the state-only residual. ## Data-use decision and boundaries

On 2026-10-31, the owner directed that the current explorer and its cited source-derived material be published for this personal/student project, and stated that the Kaggle player statistics, Transfermarkt-derived value and identity material, player images, club marks, and anonymized injury research data are acceptable for this intended use. This is the owner's project-use decision and governs this project unless the owner changes it.

That decision is recorded separately from the source's own license or terms: it is not an upstream license grant, independent legal certification, or permission for commercial use or unrelated redistribution. The source register preserves source-specific declarations, attribution, and unresolved provenance. Do not describe a source as openly licensed merely because the owner authorized its use in this project.

## 2026-11-03 WAR research execution review

The local 3/5/10-event window-availability audit was rerun successfully against the hash-verified 2017–18 Wyscout archive. It included 1,825 score-reconciled matches and 3,069,585 event states; the five-event post-action window is shortened at full time for 9,125 states (0.30%). This measures horizon coverage only, not horizon predictive quality or action value. The workstream review and six-comparison feasibility table are in [WAR research execution review](war_research_execution_2026-11-03.md). No player score, formula, or player-credit decision was made.

The same-season leave-one-league-out expected-points check was rerun with 1,000 paired match-cluster bootstrap replicates per fold. Score/time/context had lower state-weighted RMSE, Brier score, and log loss point estimates in all five held-out leagues; however, uncertainty intervals for RMSE differences crossed zero in most league folds, and mean-match RMSE intervals crossed zero in all five. This is prediction evidence only: it is not a chronological holdout, action attribution, or player-value validation. No player scores or formula/credit decisions were made. Details are in [the cross-league report](expected_points_cross_league_transfer_2026-11-02.md); the bootstrap CSV is local-only and ignored by Git.

A separate results-only pre-match baseline was fitted on 2017–18 through 2023–24 and evaluated once on 1,732 scored 2024–25 matches. Lagged five-match team form improved Brier, log loss, and expected-points RMSE versus historical league outcome rates in all five leagues. A paired league-stratified match bootstrap (2,000 replicates) gave intervals favoring the fitted baseline on all four overall metrics; this uncertainty is conditional on the fixed model and does not replace multiple chronological test seasons. Calibration diagnostics were worse on macro-class ECE for the fitted model (0.0241 vs 0.0113), with one sparse bin driving the largest discrepancy, so probability reliability remains an issue to investigate. The benchmark contains no player or event-action values and is not WAR. See [pre-match expected-points baseline](prematch_expected_points_baseline_2026-11-03.md); generated CSVs remain ignored local outputs.

The same results-only benchmark was then evaluated with seven expanding-window chronological folds, testing 2018–19 through 2024–25 and fitting each fold only on earlier seasons. Lagged form improved all four pooled metrics and all four metrics in each league in every fold; across 12,513 out-of-fold matches, both-teams expected-points RMSE was 1.25267 versus 1.30316 for historical league rates. This supports a reusable match-level baseline but remains team prediction, not individual player or event-action value. No WAR specification was selected. Details and the rerun command are in [the walk-forward report](prematch_expected_points_walkforward_2026-11-03.md); generated outputs remain local/ignored.

A COVID-training sensitivity fit compared using all prior seasons with omitting 2019–20 and 2020–21 when forecasting 2021–22 through 2024–25. Later-season metric differences were small and mixed: the all-prior lagged-form model did slightly better in three folds and worse in one. This informs only the narrow team-outcome baseline and does not decide COVID treatment for player data. COVID-era scope remains unchanged pending owner discussion. See [COVID training sensitivity](covid_training_sensitivity_2026-11-03.md); detailed outputs remain local/ignored.

The consecutive replay was extended with descriptive endpoint differences at 1, 3, 5, and 10 events on the reused March–May 2018 sample. Absolute movement rises with longer horizons, especially for the event-context model, whose predictive performance was worse in the separate cross-league check. The endpoint windows overlap and include intervening actions and clock, so they do not estimate isolated action credit or select a horizon. See the [consecutive-action report](consecutive_event_action_accounting_2026-11-02.md). The aggregate CSV is ignored/local-only; no row-level player values were generated.

An earlier adjusted-lineup feasibility pilot used the 2017–18 Wyscout season and reported a favorable held-out association at one regularization setting. That result was sensitive to model settings and has been superseded as the basis for the WAR-page summary by the eight-season OpenPlay analysis above. Preserve the earlier pilot as historical research only; it is not evidence of a stable lineup contribution or a player rating. See [the original adjusted-lineup pilot](adjusted_lineup_feasibility_2026-11-03.md) and the [multi-season lineup-presence report](openplay_lineup_presence_walkforward_2026-11-05.md).

The earlier Wyscout teammate-overlap pilot used only 2017–18 and has been superseded for exposure coverage by the eight-season OpenPlay audit above. It remains a separate historical source sample and its counts should not be pooled with OpenPlay.

The pilot design choices are recorded in [the hand-set benchmark decision note](transparent_action_value_decision.md): a transparent context table compared with fitted expected-points estimates, paired net expected-points movement across both teams, and full-transition versus clock-adjusted-residual sensitivity views.
