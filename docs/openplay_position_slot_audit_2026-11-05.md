# OpenPlay formation-slot position audit — 2026-11-05

## Decision

This audit is a promising source-evidence check, but it does **not** pass the actual-position-minutes gate for player WAR. The slot-to-label mapping predicts the OpenPlay lineup's recorded kickoff-position label very accurately in later seasons. Raw formation-slot minute totals differ from source lineup minutes for 24,272 of 372,738 held-out player-match rows by more than five minutes. A separate sensitivity analysis improves this substantially by extending a team's final formation interval to its last recorded event, but that timestamp is not an official final-whistle time and the rule has not been validated. A kickoff label is also not independent proof of every in-match role. Do not use these results to publish player scores or claim fully validated role minutes.

## Method

`scripts/audit_openplay_formation_slots.py` parses the pinned local OpenPlay v0.1.0 formation and lineup files for the Big Five, 2017–18 through 2024–25. It links each starter's lineup position label to the slot in the first formation spell, learns a modal position label for each formation-shape/slot combination, and tests that mapping with an expanding time split: each season from 2018–19 onward is evaluated using only earlier seasons. This yields 35 league-season holdouts; 2017–18 is training-only. The 20-label minimum / 95% mapping-purity rule is an audit sensitivity threshold, not an approved FutWAR position rule.

Formation records with mismatched arrays, duplicate active player IDs, invalid slots, or invalid time intervals are excluded. A broad 200-minute ceiling catches source sentinels such as 32,767; it is a data-quality guard, not a claim that actual regulation play lasts 200 minutes. The source files are not modified. Outputs are aggregate summaries only, written under ignored `reports/openplay_position_slot_audit/`; no player IDs are exported.

## Results

- 135,598 formation records were screened. 425 were excluded: 7 array-length mismatches and 418 invalid time intervals. No duplicate active-player or invalid-slot formation rows were detected after parsing.
- Of 275,165 held-out starter labels, the 20-label / 95%-purity mapping was eligible for 273,812 (99.51%). It matched 273,756 (99.98%) of eligible recorded kickoff labels. Across the 35 cells, eligible-label accuracy ranged from 99.79% to 100%; eligible coverage ranged from 97.11% upward.
- The same threshold mapped 25,834,197 of 25,973,691 held-out formation-slot player-minutes (99.46%). Cell-level mapping coverage ranged from 97.08% upward. These are minutes represented by supported formation slots, not a reconciliation to the appearance-minute denominator.
- For played player-match rows in those holdout seasons, formation-slot duration totals were within five minutes of the lineup's `minutes_played` in 348,466 of 372,738 rows (93.49%). 95,567 rows matched exactly (25.64%); 24,272 differed by more than five minutes (6.51%). The median signed difference (formation minus lineup minutes) was -2, the median absolute difference was 2, and the maximum absolute difference was 95 minutes. Missing formation entries account for 2,223 rows (24 starters and 2,199 substitutes).
- Within-five-minute reconciliation was 94.31% for starters and 91.16% for substitutes. The substitute group also had a larger median absolute difference (3 minutes versus 2 for starters), so the disagreement is not confined to one appearance type.
- The lineup's own `minutes_played` equals `minute_off - minute_on` exactly for all 372,738 held-out played rows. This confirms internal consistency of those lineup fields, but it is not an independent validation of the formation durations.
- Combining the 20-label / 95%-purity mapping with the within-five-minute reconciliation rule leaves 24,194,271 mapped slot-minutes across player-match rows that pass that tolerance. That equals 90.39% of all source lineup minutes in the holdouts; the cell-level rate ranges from 75.41% (Premier League 2023–24) to 96.33% (Serie A 2020–21). This is an exploratory coverage ceiling for source position labels, not verified FutWAR role exposure; the five-minute tolerance and mapping threshold remain diagnostic settings.
- The match table flags none of the 12,508 held-out matches as extra time, but 189 have `expanded_max_minute > 105` (maximum 129). Their 5,815 played player-match rows reconcile within five minutes only 53.59% of the time. Among the other 12,319 matches / 366,923 rows at 105 minutes or less, agreement is 94.12%. Treat the >105 group as requiring match-specific timeline review, not as 189 proven errors: six targeted examples among the most extreme values were checked, not a representative sample. Five have independent reporting of unusually long play/stoppage: Brentford–Wolves had a drone interruption and 19 first-half minutes added; Getafe–Barcelona reached 90+15 and had a reported 117-minute duration; Union Berlin–Wolfsburg had about 30 minutes of fan-protest interruptions and a 45+25 goal; Reims–Lille resumed after a roughly 35-minute injury stoppage, with a 45+30 goal; Darmstadt–Stuttgart is reported to have lasted nearly 125 minutes. Separately, Nîmes–Monaco's local event timeline reaches 129 while independent sources record its final event at 90+1; that specific value is confirmed wrong. These examples show that high expanded-minute values mix real match delays/stoppage with bad clock values. The ordinary-time group still has 21,573 rows outside the five-minute tolerance.
- The source dictionary defines `minute` as the displayed match clock and `expanded_minute` as continuous through stoppage time; it defines match length from the last event before any shootout. In the six targeted fixtures above, the largest second-half displayed and expanded minutes were: Brentford–Wolves 99/126; Getafe–Barcelona 105/116; Nîmes–Monaco 90/129; Reims–Lille 93/128; Union Berlin–Wolfsburg 95/123; Darmstadt–Stuttgart 98/124. This is consistent with the expanded clock retaining elapsed time through documented stoppages in the first five reported-interruption cases, but it does not independently validate player substitution intervals. Nîmes–Monaco's difference is inconsistent with the independently reported end at 90+1. These are six purposefully selected extreme cases, not a representative validation sample. See the bundled [OpenPlay data dictionary](../data/raw/openplay/data-v0.1.0/source_docs/DATA_DICTIONARY.md) and the cited match reports below.
- As a review aid, across the 189 held-out >105-minute matches, second-half maximum `expanded_minute` exceeded the displayed `minute` by a median of 7 minutes (range 1–39); 58 had a difference of at least 10. Eleven had a first-half maximum `expanded_minute` of 60 or later. These aggregates describe clock encoding only. They do not establish whether any individual player was present during added or interrupted time and must not be used to impute role minutes. The 2017–18 training cells contain two further >105-minute matches, so are outside this 189-match held-out count.
- Reconciliation varied sharply by cell: the lowest within-five-minute rate was 78.85% in the Premier League 2023–24 holdout; the median cell rate was 94.74%. This is too uneven to silently treat all formation durations as player exposure.
- A deliberately unapproved sensitivity grid was evaluated separately in `scripts/audit_openplay_stoppage_extension_sensitivity.py`, testing 5-, 10-, 15-, and 30-minute maximum extensions. For matches whose last recorded event was at or before minute 105 and are not flagged extra time, each team's final valid formation spell is extended only when its source end is at least minute 85 and the proposed extension is within the tested cap. At the 15-minute cap, 24,592 spells changed (71,563 aggregate minutes; maximum actual extension 14). Overall within-five-minute agreement rose from 93.49% to 98.30%; for matches with timelines at or below 105 it rose from 94.12% to 99.01%. Starter agreement rose from 94.31% to 98.38%, and substitute agreement from 91.16% to 98.07%. Under the same mapping threshold, supported slot-minutes within reconciliation tolerance rose from 90.39% to 97.80% of source lineup minutes (98.58% for matches at or below minute 105). The 10-minute cap is slightly lower: 98.27% overall and 97.76% supported mapped minutes. This pattern makes final-interval stoppage time a plausible source of much disagreement, not proof that every inferred minute is correct. The last event is only a proxy for full time; it may precede the actual whistle, and incomplete timelines may be wrong in either direction. The 189 matches above minute 105 are left unchanged pending match-specific clock/segment review. They include both long legitimate interruptions and at least one confirmed bad source clock, so they should not be described or handled as a single error class. Do not apply any sensitivity to canonical data or player scores without independent validation. Full comparison, caveats, and thresholds are in the [stoppage-extension sensitivity report](openplay_stoppage_extension_sensitivity_2026-11-05.md).
- One inspected anomaly is Nîmes–Monaco on 1 February 2020: the local match event timeline reaches minute 129 while the formation spans stop at 90; independent match reporting records a Ligue 1 3–1 result and a 90+1' final event, confirming that this particular 129-minute clock is erroneous. This spot check does not validate the other 188 anomalous matches. [L'Équipe match report](https://www.lequipe.fr/Football/match-direct/ligue-1/2019-2020/nimes-monaco-live/451185) and the [French Football Federation disciplinary report](https://media.fff.fr/uploads/document/c426634af71079b5d2d93a18edae78b2.pdf).
- Sixteen source position labels occur in the linked starter sample: `GK, DC, DL, DR, DMC, DML, DMR, MC, ML, MR, AMC, AML, AMR, FW, FWL, FWR`. No mapping to FutWAR's 14 position codes is asserted here. In particular, `FW` and wing labels do not decide ST/CAM or wide-back classifications.

These results support the narrower claim that OpenPlay formation-slot shapes are highly predictive of their linked source kickoff labels in a forward-season check. They do not validate continuous role changes, every player's tactical assignment, or an identity link into the player-season statistical panel. The five-minute tolerance is a diagnostic cut, not a correction rule.

## Reproduce

From the repository root in Windows PowerShell:

```powershell
py scripts\audit_openplay_formation_slots.py
py scripts\audit_openplay_stoppage_extension_sensitivity.py
```

The detailed outputs are local and ignored. This script reads raw source files but writes only the derived audit summaries.

## Remaining position-minute work

1. Validate the final-spell stoppage-time sensitivity grid against independent final-whistle/substitution evidence on a stratified sample; investigate the 189 >105-minute matches and residual cell-specific mismatches, distinguishing legitimate interrupted/prolonged play from malformed event clocks. Do not treat the sensitivity as a correction without that check.
2. Preserve unmatched or discrepant player-match minutes as unknown rather than assigning a slot.
3. Validate that inferred role spells, including players who switch slots, map correctly to the owner's approved role codes against an independent role source or a sufficiently audited sample.
4. Reconcile event-player identities to the player-season panel, and keep goalkeeper evidence on its separate calibration path.

Until those checks are complete, this is a position-evidence prototype and not a released position-minute dataset.
